## ST-05b Referee request email from Finalise decisions: 6-9 h

**What it is.** When the process owner finalises decisions, the system emails the referee to ask for their reference or confirmation. No accepted task does this ([plan row ST-05b](../../docs/plans/stress-test-feedback-2026-11-plan.md#L82)).
**Comparables.** `wbs:3.2` trigger flow (4-6 h) and `wbs:6.6` finalise-decisions flow (3-4 h). This is a smaller flow than 3.2 but needs template, guards and a test round.
**Breakdown (hours).** Email content and template with Emily's review 1-2; flow step or child flow with referee-details guard, send-once guard and failure handling 2-3; sent-record on the grant so it cannot go twice 0.5-1; test across approved, partial and missing-referee cases 1.5-2; walkthrough and one round of changes 1-1.5.
**Assumptions.**
- Referee name and email are on the grant at the moment of sending. A-15 FR-122 shows they are currently often empty when the grant row is created; if they are filled later, the trigger moves to a manual send like FR-122 and this range holds, but a prompt-to-collect step would add 2-3 h.
- One email per grant, plain text with a link or reply address, no reply tracking, no reminders, no referee-facing portal or form.
- Sent through the existing mailbox or connection the system already uses; no new licence or connector.
- The referee sees only what the DocuSign decision already allows (the referee sees all applicant details per the standing decision); the email adds no new personal data beyond that.

## ST-12b Historical demographics import: 14-26 h

**What it is.** Load earlier years' applicant demographics into the system so statistics and the register reflect history ([plan row ST-12b](../../docs/plans/stress-test-feedback-2026-11-plan.md#L95)).
**Comparables.** `wbs:4.2` field mapping (2-3 h) plus a one-off load and reconciliation, with cleansing as the unknown. No accepted task imports history.
**Breakdown (hours).** Source inspection, profiling and field mapping 3-5; transformation and cleansing rules, including inconsistent categories and duplicates 3-6; import script or flow, idempotent and re-runnable 3-5; validation and reconciliation against source counts, with a sample reviewed by Emily 2-4; rollback and re-run procedure plus handbook note 1-2; one rehearsal in DEV and one in ACC 2-4.
**Assumptions.**
- One structured source (a spreadsheet or export), under about 2,000 rows (the reviewer's figure of about 1,500 applications), consistent enough to map in one pass.
- Demographics only. No narratives, no free text, no attachments, no bank or referee data.
- Loaded into the anonymised statistics table or an equivalent, not into live application records, so no workflow or scoring runs against historical rows.
- Source supplied by the client; hours do not include chasing it or re-keying paper records. Paper or scanned sources would take this above the range and need re-pricing.

**GATE, NOT BUILD WORK: needs its own lawful basis and data-protection decision before it can be built.** Old demographics are special-category data in places (disability, ethnicity and similar, per SDD §7.1 and FR-016 and FR-031). Applicants gave it for an application, and nothing in the repository shows they were told it would be reused for statistics. The plan row says the same: "old special-category data would need its own classification and lawful basis." Before any build hours start, the data-protection owner and the reviewer need to settle: the lawful basis and the Article 9 condition for this reuse; whether the data can be anonymised before it reaches the system (which changes the build and may reduce the hours); retention; and whether a data-protection impact assessment is needed. No hours are estimated for that decision. The range above assumes the answer permits a load of anonymised or already-aggregated figures. If it requires row-level special-category data, add 4-8 h for access controls, retention hooks and erasure coverage, and re-price.

## ST-14 Trustee rotation table and automated review creation: 14-22 h

**What it is.** Today the process owner creates each review by hand and picks the two trustees. This stores who is on rotation and creates the reviews automatically when applications are released ([plan row ST-14](../../docs/plans/stress-test-feedback-2026-11-plan.md#L97)). It builds on FR-123 and FR-124 and the two-trustee rule of A-14.
**Comparables.** `wbs:0.4` schema (5-8 h for several tables, about 1-2 h per simple table), `wbs:6.4` decision capture (3-5 h), `wbs:6.6` flow (3-4 h), `wbs:0.5` security (4-6 h across three roles).
**Breakdown (hours).** Rotation table (trustee, active window, order or weight, availability) with security role access 2-3; admin screen to maintain it 2-3; rotation logic choosing two trustees per application with the rules below 3-5; flow that creates reviews on release, idempotent, with failure notice to the process owner 3-4; migration of existing manual reviews so nothing is duplicated 1-2; tests including absent trustee and odd trustee counts 2-3; walkthrough and one round of changes 1-2.
**Assumptions.**
- The rule is simple: two trustees per application, rotating evenly through the active ones, skipping anyone marked unavailable. Conflict-of-interest checks, weighting by region or workload and trustee self-service availability are out.
- The process owner can still override or add a review manually.
- The trustee portal and its verdict rules stay as built; only review creation is automated.
- OQ-065 (default for who sees what) stays as answered by the reviewer; if the answer changes, re-price.
- This range excludes any rule not stated above. The rotation rules are not yet written down, which is why confidence is low-medium: Emily needs to confirm them before a firm figure.
