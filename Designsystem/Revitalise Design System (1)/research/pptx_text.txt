--- Slide 28 ---
Describing our journey | Our history | Founded as The Winged Fellowship Trust in 1963, we provided unique respite holidays for over 60 years. Since, 1996, when we were renamed  | Revitalise | , we have provided over 42,000 respite breaks to people in real need. However, in November 2024, we were left with no option but to close our holiday  | centres |  due to the huge increases in the costs of providing our breaks and the reductions in available respite funding from Local Authorities and grant-makers. | Despite this, we were determined to continue the legacy of our Founder, Joan Brander, in ensuring that disabled people and carers continue to enjoy the breaks and holidays they need. Caring for a loved one can be relentless and exhausting and without respite, many carers reach burnout. For disabled people needing care support, accessing appropriate holidays, social activities and opportunities to make social connections can be extremely challenging, leaving many disabled people experiencing loneliness and social isolation. | Today, our vital work focuses on providing grants to enable disabled adults and their family carers to enjoy the respite breaks, holidays and social connections they need. 

--- Slide 29 ---
And what we do  | What we do | We provide funding for respite breaks and holidays, social activities like day trips, and opportunities for people to meet and make social connections. Any disabled person or carer over the age of 18 can apply for a grant towards a break or experience that would make a meaningful difference to them.  | How it works | Applications are open year-round and each month we have a maximum amount of grants we can distribute. This is because as a small charity there is a maximum number of grants we can give out each year. We give priority to those who have not had a grant before or in the last 12 months, but welcome repeat applications.  | We also welcome applications from groups who wish to holiday or take a trip together.

Background | UK Trust and foundation fundraising will be highly competitive in 2026 due to increased demand for services and the economic pressures of the "big squeeze," which are driving up costs and creating greater competition for limited funding. This perfect storm is made worse by the winding down of one-off COVID-19 funds, higher application numbers, and the lingering effects of austerity and the cost-of-living crisis, causing success rates to fall significantly.

Pipeline work – methodology and results | We have removed funders no longer relevant from the existing T&F pipeline. | All previously warm funders and any who have not donated in more than 24 months are considered ‘lapsed’.  | Any new funders or those who have not donated before are considered cold. | We have set success rates at 9% for cold and 20% for lapsed. This is in line with 2023 success rates and sits below the sector benchmarks.  | No consideration has been given towards new potential funders being sourced via further research – important given the change of the charity’s size and activities. | This analysis work produces a sum for 2026/27 in excess of what has been assumed in the forecast. This is because whether or not Revitalise will be an attractive proposition as a grant maker is not proven.  | This is also why we have not considered sector benchmarks as relevant yet even though  | Revitalise’s |  previous avg success rate (32%) was in excess of the sector benchmark (26%). | Work on this pipeline is ongoing and will be built into the first reforecast of the year but thus far demonstrates that the £180k 3-year figure is realistic.

--- Slide 42 ---
3 year forecast - methodology | £50k is a reasonable number to raise in year 1 based on previous performance and the Gifted Philanthropy research. No cost or ROI factored in due to need to upweight resource for the first six months to get fundraising going | We would expect to at least double that in year 2, based on previous performance and the level of resource dedicated to fundraising, which will remain high. | By year 3 our aim is to be at optimum income and ROI level. We have based this both on our historic fundraising efforts and an analysis of The Respite Association’s fundraising, which was £182k for 2024 and has grown consistently over the last few years. | Resourcing is based on Trust and Grants Manager role at £60k per annum including on-costs, spending 50% of their time on Fundraising | The ROI is at the lower end for T&G fundraising but remains respectable.
