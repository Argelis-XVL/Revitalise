02

Where you work

One app, one area, three groups. You will spend most of a working day in Casework — the four queues below are filtered views of the same Applications table, each surfacing a different reason a case needs a look.

ScreenGroupWhat you'll find there
ApplicationsCasework Every case, and the active ones — your general working list.
Borderline – Awaiting ReviewCasework Scored inside the review band. The score alone didn't decide the case; you do.
Under Review – Incomplete ScoringCasework Answers were missing, so scoring couldn't run at all. Needs manual eyes before it can move anywhere.
Auto-rejected ApplicationsCasework Below the knockout line. Parked here in case a decision needs revisiting, not for routine action.
ApplicantsCasework The people behind the applications — one record even if someone has applied more than once.
SettingsConfiguration Score thresholds, the Likert point map, the redaction confidence bar, the income ceiling. Yours to tune — no flow needs editing to change how the system judges a case.
Error LogOperations Anything that failed silently would land here instead — check it if a Teams alert told you to.
03

Walking a case through

Eight stages, one case. Each card splits what runs on its own from what is waiting on a decision only you can make.

1

Submitted

Trigger — the applicant submits the form on the website.

Runs on its own
  • Case is created and given a reference
  • Matched to an existing applicant by name and email, or a new one is created
  • You get a Teams notification with the applicant's name and reference
  • A prior-grant check runs quietly against QuickBooks
Needs you
  • Nothing yet — read the notification if you want to, no action required
2

Scored

Trigger — the case record is created.

Runs on its own
  • Circumstance score calculated out of 60 from the wellbeing answers
  • Income checked against the ceiling in Settings
  • Clearly above the line → Auto-pass
  • Clearly below it → Auto-reject
  • In the middle → Borderline, routed to you
  • Answers missing → Under Review, routed to you
Needs you
  • Nothing for Auto-pass / Auto-reject unless you choose to override one
  • Borderline and Under Review cases wait in their queues for you
3

Your review

Trigger — you open a case from the Borderline or Under Review queue.

Runs on its own
  • Nothing — the system deliberately stops here and waits for a named decision
Needs you
  • Read the score breakdown, condition profile and financial answers
  • Override the automatic verdict if the score alone doesn't tell the whole story — your name and reason are recorded against it
  • Mark the case Eligible for Panel once it's ready to go in front of the trustees
4

Narrative check

Trigger — a case becomes Eligible for Panel.

Runs on its own
  • Free-text answers are scanned and names, places and identifying details replaced with labels
  • Ages become age bands, places become regions
  • High-confidence redactions are released to trustees automatically
Needs you
  • Low-confidence redactions land with you for a manual read before release — until then, trustees see none of that case's narrative
5

Panel decides

Trigger — the monthly board meeting, outside the system.

Runs on its own
  • An anonymised pack — score, holiday details, redacted narrative — can be generated per case ahead of the meeting
Needs you
  • Trustees record their verdict per case, in the portal or from the offline pack
  • After the meeting, you run Finalise Decisions once — it applies every verdict to the whole batch and creates a grant for each approval
6

Acceptance

Trigger — a case becomes Approved.

Runs on its own
  • A DocuSign envelope is built and sent for signature — applicant first, then referee or GP
  • Reminders go out at 3 and 7 days if unsigned
  • An escalation reaches you at 14 days with the applicant's details
  • Once both parties sign, the PDF is filed automatically
Needs you
  • Only if a paper print-sign-scan route is used instead — you record that one directly on the grant record
7

Payment

Trigger — the signed agreement is on file.

Runs on its own
  • A last prior-grant check re-runs before issue
Needs you / finance
  • Finance staff record the payment — this is the one step they, not you, own
8

Off the board

Trigger — an applicant withdraws, or a case never completes.

Runs on its own
  • Retention starts counting from whichever status a case ends on — nothing to schedule
Needs you
  • You record Withdrawn or Incomplete — there is no automatic path to either
04

The monthly panel

The board meeting itself happens outside the system — this is the one deliberate pause in an otherwise automated line. Everything either side of it is built to make that single meeting as well-informed as possible: a daily count of what's piling up, an anonymised pack per case, and one controlled action — Finalise Decisions — that turns a room's worth of verdicts into applied outcomes in one auditable run. Running it twice over an already-finalised round is safe; it simply does nothing the second time.

05

When something needs you

You don't need to watch the system continuously — it comes to you, on Teams, when it needs a decision or a fix.

Teams · daily
A one-line count of what was scored, auto-rejected and left Borderline in the last day. Names never appear in it.
Teams · on failure
Anything that breaks mid-process alerts you immediately and writes a row to the Error Log — nothing fails silently.
Teams · day 14
An acceptance envelope still unsigned after two reminders escalates to you by name, with the applicant's details attached.
In-app
A narrative the redaction step wasn't confident about sits withheld from trustees until you review and release it.
06

Who else touches a case

Three people can act on a case, and each one sees a deliberately different slice of it.

Process owner
REV Admin

Full casework: everything except bank details. Reads the raw narrative before redaction; owns Settings.

Finance staff
REV Finance

Bank details and payments only — no access to an applicant's health or circumstances at all.

Trustee
REV Trustee

Reads redacted cases through the portal and records a verdict — never the identity behind one.
